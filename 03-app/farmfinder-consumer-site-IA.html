FarmFinder App — Website-First

Consumer Site — Information Architecture & Wireframe Skeleton

Planning artifact · 2026-07-13 · Scope: Discovery core + Trust/story layer + Ordering + Farm onboarding · Geography: continental-U.S. product, South-Louisiana-first rollout · Archetype: Field Journal (× Product OS) · Derived with the ui-design-system skill.

Read me first — this is a plan, not a build. Nothing here is production code. The gray boxes are low-fidelity wireframes (structure and hierarchy only — no real styling, copy, or imagery). The document chrome carries the intended brand palette so you can feel the direction, but the screens themselves are deliberately unstyled so we argue about structure before anyone writes CSS. Purpose: get aligned on pages, components, and flows for the "website-first" version of the FarmFinder app — the thing you can show users to convey the idea. Ordering and farmer-onboarding are included in the skeleton per your scope decision, even though AGENTS.md keeps them for a later build phase.

1 · Design philosophy — why it won't look like the dashboard

The internal dashboard is a facade over a spreadsheet: tabs, tables, stat cards. Correct for you; wrong for the public. The consumer site has to make someone feel that fresh, local food is all around them and worth the trip.

Three ideas drive every decision below:

  1. Place over product. The hero of this site is the region and its farms, not a search bar on a gradient. The emotional pitch ("the backbone of this country is a 20-minute drive away") is structural, not a footer afterthought.
  2. Map is the spine, story is the skin. Discovery (map + filter + profiles) is the utility that keeps people coming back; editorial (why-local, farmer spotlights, seasonality) is what converts a first-time visitor into a believer. Both are first-class.
  3. Depth is the differentiator — so surface it. Your database goes deep where LocalHarvest/Localize go shallow. The UI must show that depth: market days, what's in season this week, certifications, CSA, ships-vs-pickup — not just a pin and a name.
Anti-slop guard rails (from the ui-design-system skill) applied throughout: no left-copy/right-image hero, no eyebrow+subhead+two-button stack, no equal 3-column icon cards, no purple gradients, no Inter-for-everything, no fade-up-on-everything. The layout should be recognizable with the text stripped out.

2 · Art direction

Archetype: Field Journal (primary) × Product OS (functional screens)

Field Journal is the craft/artisan/sustainability archetype — it reads as a naturalist's field notebook: full-bleed land and produce photography, a hand-annotated feel, seasonal margins, ink-on-kraft texture. It is the correct structural metaphor for a farm-discovery brand and the single biggest lever against generic output. The transactional screens (discover, shop, dashboard) borrow Product OS structure — quiet panels, a real map, low but intentional density — so utility never fights the mood.

Palette — described in materials, not hex

Typography

Oversized editorial serif display (headlines cross imagery, journal-style); small-caps, wide-tracked humanist sans for navigation, labels, and utility. Letter-spacing modulates density: titles tight, nav wide, meta tracked. No Inter-for-everything.

Buttons & motion

Primary CTA is a single discreet transparent glass/ink pill with a fine border and uppercase small type — never two bright blocks. Motion is archetype-specific: page-curl hint and ink-bleed reveals on scroll for editorial sections, slow map/label settling for discovery — never generic fade-up. Always respect prefers-reduced-motion.

3 · Sitemap

Two audiences on one platform: eaters (public discovery + ordering) and farmers (claim + manage). Public site defaults to South Louisiana where coverage is deepest, with a region switcher wired for state-by-state expansion (Gulf South launch → adjacent regions → continental U.S.) from your scaling document.

Design principle across flows: discovery works with zero login and zero ordering. Auth is required only to save, order, or manage a farm. The offline-farm reality means the map and profiles must be fully useful even when a farm has no store, no site, and no phone — the graceful-degradation path is the product, not an edge case.

Open questions that change the build

QuestionWhy it matters / who bears the risk
Is v1 a demo/marketing site or a functional discovery tool?Demo can fake the map with curated data; functional needs the DB wired + normalized. Big cost delta — decide before build-plan.
Ordering: native vs. link-out to a farm's Square/Barn2Door?Native = liability, payments, support burden you own. Link-out ships in days and dodges marketplace logistics. Recommend link-out for v1.
Who edits a profile before a farm claims it?Trust + legal. "Listed from public records" needs a correction/opt-out path (shown in 5.3) and a policy behind it.
Seasonality model — source of truth?SeasonBand is a differentiator but must be regionally accurate (LSU AgCenter planting guides) or it erodes trust.
Does /claim launch with the first public release or after directory search is stable?Launching later reduces authorization risk; launching earlier improves farm corrections. Decide from pilot readiness.
Recommendation for the next step. This IA is deliberately broad because you asked to see the whole idea. Before any build, narrow v1 to Discovery core + Story layer + Claim, treat ordering as link-out, and keep the ring/region framing as copy, not engineering. That gives you a showable, honest "website-first" FarmFinder grounded in real data, defers the expensive marketplace machinery, and still demonstrates the full vision. When you're ready, this maps cleanly onto the product-spec → architecture → build-plan chain.
